Features

  • Arrow keys and play/pause: control videos and presentations without leaving your seat.
  • Floating remote: a button you can place anywhere on the screen or dock in the top bar; tap it for the D-pad.
  • Trackpad: move the cursor; tap to click, two-finger double-tap to right-click.
  • Gestures: two-finger scroll with momentum, long-press to drag, and three-finger swipe for desktops and Mission Control.
  • Keyboard: type on your iPhone and send the text straight to your Mac.
  • Any language and emoji: send text in any script, emoji included.
  • Spotlight: opens Spotlight on your Mac by sending ⌘ Space, then type your search from the iPhone. A custom Spotlight shortcut isn't supported.
  • Direct and encrypted: connects over Bluetooth, with no Wi-Fi setup or accounts.
  • Free: no ads, no tracking, no in-app purchases.

Installation

iPhone

The iPhone app will be on the App Store soon.

Mac

  1. Download LazyRemote.dmg.
  2. Open LazyRemote.dmg.
  3. Drag LazyRemote into the Applications folder.

Quickstart

  1. On your Mac, open LazyRemote.
  2. When macOS asks for Bluetooth access, select Allow.
  3. Make sure that the LazyRemote icon comes into view in the menu bar.
  4. Click the LazyRemote icon.
  5. Select Open Accessibility Settings….
  6. Set LazyRemote to on.
  7. On your iPhone, open LazyRemote.
  8. When iOS asks for Bluetooth access, select Allow.
  9. On your Mac, click the LazyRemote icon in the menu bar.
  10. Select Allow followed by the name of your iPhone.
  11. Make sure that the iPhone shows the name of your Mac with a green dot.
  12. Use the trackpad and the buttons below it to control your Mac. To type, tap Type to send and then select the send button.

Troubleshooting

Buttons do nothing on the Mac

LazyRemote needs Accessibility access to send key presses and clicks. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility and make sure LazyRemote is on. If it already is, turn it off and on again, then restart LazyRemote.

The iPhone can't find the Mac

  • Make sure Bluetooth is on for both devices, and that both apps have Bluetooth permission (iPhone: Settings › LazyRemote; Mac: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Bluetooth).
  • Make sure LazyRemote is running and enabled in the Mac menu bar.
  • On the iPhone, tap the … menu and select Reconnect.

The connection is refused after reinstalling

For security, LazyRemote refuses a Mac or iPhone whose identity has changed. Only after verifying the intended device, select Forget Mac in the iPhone's … menu, select Remove next to the iPhone in the Mac menu bar, and then pair again.
